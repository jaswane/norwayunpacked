### Second research pass — 2026-08-12 (editorial polish)

S16 was opened and parsed in full during the polish sprint, together with a
re-check of S11 (petroleum revenue components) and S7 (value breakdown). New
verified material from that pass:

- **Fund spending should be below the expected return in normal times.** S16:
  "This implies that fund spending should be below the expected real return in
  good and normal times. In previous analyses, the Ministry of Finance has
  estimated that spending 2.7 per cent of the fund in normal times, over time
  results in a fund spending that correspond to the expected real return of 3
  percent." This explains *why* the 2026 figure is 2.7 percent and is now the
  third point in the article's fiscal-rule section.
- **Two different deficit figures must not be conflated.** S16: "The non-oil
  deficit is the actual transfer from the Government Pension Fund Global to the
  fiscal budget and is estimated to NOK 452.2 billion next year", while the
  structural non-oil deficit for 2026 is NOK 579.4 billion. The earlier version
  of the article called the 579 figure "the transfer"; that was imprecise and
  has been corrected. 579 = the adjusted (structural) measure the fiscal rule
  applies to; 452 = the actual money moved.
- **State budget expenditure 2026: NOK 2,200,561 million** (S16, table on
  underlying expenditure growth).
- **Four petroleum revenue components, 2026 estimates** (S11): tax 386.1bn,
  net cash flow from SDFI 262.8bn, Equinor dividend 25.4bn, environmental taxes
  and area fees 11.3bn (CO2 tax 9.9 + area fees 1.4); net cash flow 686bn. The
  earlier version said "three streams" and omitted environmental taxes/area fees.
- **SDFI in plain English** (S11): "The State's Direct Financial Interest (SDFI)
  is a system under which the Norwegian state owns holdings in a number of oil
  and gas fields, pipelines and onshore facilities"; the state covers its share
  of investments and costs and receives its share of the income.
- **Value-component rounding** (S7): 15,210 + 5,509 + 1,965 = 22,684 against a
  reported total of 22,683. NBIM publishes no rounding footnote, so the article
  states the observation itself rather than adjusting any official figure.

### Interpretation (our explanation, grounded in the above)

- Because the fund is a krone deposit whose countervalue is invested in global markets, calling it "a bank account full of oil money" is misleading (grounded in S1 §2, S2 §1-1, S5).
- Because more than half the value is accumulated return (S7), the fund is now primarily a compounding investment portfolio rather than a pile of oil revenue.
- Because spending is tied to expected real return rather than to a fixed annual withdrawal, and because the 2026 figure is 2.7 percent rather than exactly 3 percent (S10), "Norway may spend 3 percent every year" is an inaccurate formulation.
- Because the capital is not earmarked (S5) and reaches people only via the state budget after a Storting resolution (S1 §5), no individual has a personal claim on a share of the fund.
- Because the fund transfer (NOK 579 bn) is a large but partial part of state finances alongside taxes and other revenue, "the Oil Fund pays for the welfare state" overstates the relationship.

### Arithmetic performed by us (not a sourced claim)

- NOK 22,683 billion ÷ 5,633,770 residents ≈ **NOK 4.0 million per resident**. Presented explicitly as division, not as an entitlement. Inputs: S6/S7 and S13.
- Net petroleum inflow 2026 (NOK 686 bn, S11) exceeds the 2026 transfer to the budget (NOK 579 bn, S10), so the fund is still receiving more petroleum revenue than the state withdraws. Stated as a comparison of two official estimates for the same year.

### Cultural observation (not presented as documented fact)

- "Oljefondet" is an everyday word in Norwegian public conversation. Framed as a broad cultural observation in the Andreas note, not as a measured fact.

- **"About 27 percent of the central government budget is financed by transfers
  from the fund" — investigated and NOT used.** The figure appears in third-party
  summaries, but it could not be found in the primary documents we opened (S16,

  no longer the headline in the welfare-state section, because it is hard for a
  general reader to interpret. It must never be described as a share of the
  state budget.

  the explanation and created avoidable F3 maintenance. NBIM as an institution is
  still explained.
- **Long official quotations replaced with plain-English paraphrase** in most
  places; short quotes kept only where exact wording carries weight.
- **Second Andreas placeholder removed.** The editorial system must not require a
  personal anecdote in every article; one genuine cultural observation is enough.
- **Heading changes** for accuracy and tone: "Why none of it is invested in
  Norway" → "Why the fund doesn't invest directly in Norway" (the mandate permits
  Norwegian legal entities to hold foreign assets); "What happens when the oil and
  gas run out?" → "What happens as Norway's petroleum revenues decline?" (gradual,
  not abrupt); "What people most often get wrong" → "What the Oil Fund is — and
